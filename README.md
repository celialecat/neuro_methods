# What Does It Mean to Understand a Complex System?

### A literature review and position paper at the intersection of philosophy of science, systems science, complexity science, information theory, neuroscience, and mechanistic interpretability

---

This repository contains a comprehensive research deliverable structured around a single
epistemological question raised by Jonas & Kording (2017, *Could a Neuroscientist Understand
a Microprocessor?*) and Lazebnik (2002, *Could a Biologist Fix a Radio?*):

> **What does it actually mean to understand a complex system, and how can we tell whether a
> scientific method produces understanding rather than merely prediction, compression,
> description, or correlation?**

## Central thesis

Throughout, we maintain an explicit distinction between:

- **Methods that produce *knowledge about* a system** — statistical regularities, predictive
  mappings, accurate representations, correlations, descriptions; and
- **Methods that produce *understanding of* a system** — recovery of mechanisms, causal
  structure, hierarchical/modular organization, invariants, and generative principles, together
  with the *competences* (counterfactual, interventional, repair, redesign, transfer) that such
  recovery confers.

For every framework and method reviewed, we identify the **epistemic object** it recovers
(statistical regularity, causal relation, mechanism, abstraction, hierarchy, invariant,
generative principle) and ask whether that object is *sufficient* for scientific understanding.

## How to read this

- For the **argument and the proposed framework**, read `paper/position_paper.md` first, then
  `parts/part6_framework.md` and `parts/part4_criteria.md`.
- For the **scholarly grounding**, read Parts I–III.
- For the **operationalization**, read Parts IV–VII and the tables.
- For **the application target** (neuroscience and interpretability), read Part VIII.
- For the **original theoretical contribution and reflections** (the ι-machine, the indeterminacy
  theorem, the Genesis Principle, the understanding horizon), read `parts/part9_deep_theory.md`, then
  the distilled `paper/essay_genesis_of_intelligibility.md`.
