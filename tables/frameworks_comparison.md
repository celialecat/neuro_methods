# Comparison Table of Frameworks of Scientific Understanding/Explanation

Columns:
- **Epistemic object** — what the framework says understanding/explanation *recovers*.
- **Conception** — ontic (in the world) / epistemic (in the agent) / modal.
- **Graded?** — does it make understanding a matter of degree?
- **Interventional?** — does it require/define interventions?
- **Hierarchy?** — does it centrally involve levels/scales?
- **K vs U** — does the framework, by itself, mark *knowledge-about* vs *understanding-of*?
- **Key limitation.**

## How this project's framework relates (one row)

| **UKG / mechanistic-understanding framework (this work, Part VI)** | gated profile over {F,CF,CC,HC,MO,K,T,R,Δ,X,P,IV,B}; commuting modeling relation; epistemic ladder 0–6; `(KA,UO)` coordinates | ontic (object) + epistemic (competence) | **yes** (degree d) | **yes** (central) | **yes** (decomposability profile) | **yes** (KA vs UO is the explicit axis) | thresholds/weights are domain-set; estimators benchmark-specific |

**Reading the table.** The frameworks that *mark* the knowledge/understanding distinction and are
*graded + interventional + hierarchy-aware* (Woodward, Craver, Strevens, Simon, RG, Pearl, causal
abstraction, causal emergence, CRL) are exactly the ones our framework synthesizes; the ones that
*fail to mark it* (Hempel, van Fraassen, IB, raw AIT/MDL) are the ones whose adoption licenses the
prediction-as-understanding error this project targets.
